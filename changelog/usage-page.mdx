Knowing your credit balance is useful. Knowing what consumed it is what actually lets you act. The usage page in **Settings** > **Usage** now answers both: a daily chart of what you spent, four ways to slice it, and warning emails before you hit the bottom.

## Credits by day

The top of the page shows total credits for the period, split into **LLM** (tokens models consume reading your prompts and writing responses) and **Compute** (resources that run your requests end to end). Below it, a stacked bar chart plots the same split day by day, so a spike is immediately visible and immediately dated.

The date range defaults to the last 30 days and goes back up to 90.

## Four ways to slice it

**Group by** switches the breakdown table between four views. Each row carries an activity sparkline, chat count, credits, and share of the period's total.

- **Chat** — every chat with its creator, source, last used time, and credits. Sort by **Top usage** to find the expensive ones or **Last used** to find the recent ones.
- **User** — credits per person on the team.
- **Source** — where the work came from: Web, Slack, GitHub, Linear, iMessage, MCP, Extension, or Automation. Scheduled tasks and webhooks both roll up as **Automation**, so all unattended work sits in one row.
- **Intelligence Level** — credits by Super Fast, Fast, Balanced, and Deep, so you can see how much of the bill comes from the heaviest reasoning.

Admins see the whole organization. Members see their own chats.

## Know before you run out

The credits card on the billing page now leads with what's left in plain terms: the percentage still available and the exact figure, as in "29% credits available — 8,790,438 of 30,002,500 credits". The bar turns amber past 75% used and red past 90%.

Adapt emails owners and admins at those same two marks, once each per billing period, so a quiet month of automations doesn't become a surprise. Every email links straight to the usage page.
